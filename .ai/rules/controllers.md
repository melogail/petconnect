## Authorization is $this->authorize() in the controller — never in a Form Request
App\Http\Controllers\Controller uses AuthorizesRequests. **Every action that acts on a policy-governed model calls `$this->authorize()`** against that model's policy — including the public ones, where the policy method takes `?User` and returns true (PetPolicy::viewAny for the home feed, PetPolicy::view for a listing page). A guest-visible page should be a recorded decision in a policy, not the absence of a check.

Actions with no policy-governed model do not call it. The Settings controllers act on the authenticated user, which route middleware already establishes, and there is no second party whose access is in question; adding a policy there would be ceremony. That is the only exemption — if an action loads, writes or exposes a model somebody else could own, it authorizes.

Form Requests validate only; do not implement authorize() in them, and do not use Gate::authorize() in controllers.

Why: the legacy app split the same decision three ways — StorePetRequest::authorize() returned true while its controller never called the policy (anyone could publish), UpdatePetRequest checked the policy itself, and PetController::edit checked nothing at all (any verified account could read another owner's vet details, exact coordinates and medications). One convention, one place to audit.
